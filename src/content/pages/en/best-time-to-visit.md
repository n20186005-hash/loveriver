## When to arrive

Do not wait until the sky is completely dark. Arriving 45–60 minutes before sunset gives you time to scout the river, bridges, pedestrian traffic and a safe camera position. About half an hour after sunset, blue hour preserves detail in the sky while streetlights and building lights begin to glow.

## Three scenes worth photographing

### Reflections in the downtown river

The narrower downtown banks frame bridges and illuminated buildings, especially when wind is low and the water is still. Keep tripods out of the main pedestrian route.

### Sunset over the harbor

Glory Pier and the open waterfront around Kaohsiung Music Center allow compositions with buildings, water and sky. Summer afternoons can be intensely hot with little shade: bring water.

### Light trails from sightseeing boats

A slower shutter can capture boats passing across the water; phones can use night mode. Stay inside safety barriers and never lean over the edge for a photograph.

## Seasons and weather

Winter is generally more comfortable for extended walks. Summer sunsets happen later, but afternoon heat, thunderstorms and typhoon-related weather can affect plans. Spring and autumn often make it easier to enjoy blue hour at a relaxed pace. Festival lighting can be spectacular but crowds reduce room for photography; ordinary weekday evenings are quieter.

## Five phone photography tips

1. Clean the camera lens before shooting lights.
2. Tap a bright light source and lower exposure slightly to avoid blown highlights.
3. Stabilize the phone without extending it beyond a railing.
4. Take one frame with more sky and another emphasizing reflections.
5. Finish photography before lowering screen brightness to enjoy the walk.

## What if it rains?

Wet pavement can create striking reflections after light rain. During thunderstorms, strong winds or waterfront restrictions, move away from the water. An indoor exhibition around the music center or Pier-2 can provide an alternative until conditions improve.
