## Four decisions that make the visit easier

Facilities are distributed along a long open waterfront rather than gathered at one visitor center. Plan the essentials before arriving.

| Decide | Recommended approach | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where to park | Park near an MRT station and transfer | Prime waterfront spaces fill quickly during evenings and events |
| Where to find toilets | Identify stations, shopping centers and public parking buildings | Smaller park toilets may have limited operating hours |
| Water and sun protection | Buy supplies in the surrounding city blocks | Shade and drinking-water points are limited on exposed banks |
| When to eat | Walk inland for a wider restaurant choice | Waterfront food outlets can be busy at peak times |

## Facilities by type

### Restrooms and drinking water

MRT and light-rail connections, public cultural venues and larger shopping buildings are useful reference points. Availability and opening times vary; locate the next usable facility before embarking on a longer walk. Carry your own drinking water, particularly in hot weather.

### Parking and public transport

Use legal public garages and signed spaces; check height limits, opening hours and payment methods. Parking near major events can be restricted. For most visitors, Orange Line MRT plus the light rail is simpler than moving a car between waterfront sections.

### Food, shops and supplies

The Cianjin and Yancheng street grids offer restaurants, convenience stores and everyday supplies. Commercial services immediately on the riverfront may be fewer or busier, particularly after performances. Check each business's current opening hours.

### Accommodation

Cianjin makes the classic river walk convenient, while Yancheng connects with Pier-2 and the harbor. If early rail or HSR departures matter, consider staying near the appropriate station. See the [accommodation guide](/en/hotels/).

### Fuel and electric-vehicle charging

Petrol stations are in the surrounding urban road network rather than on pedestrian waterfronts. Public parking facilities and shopping-center garages may have EV charging, often with separate parking charges. Verify live charger availability and the provider's payment process before driving there.

### Accessible routes

Some paths are broad and level, but levees, bridges and street crossings can have ramps, steps or temporary closures. Check the exact accessible entrance and allow a detour when needed. Keep boarding and bridge approaches clear for wheelchairs and strollers.

### Information and visitor assistance

There is no single universal visitor desk for the entire river. Ask at transit customer-service counters, museums and event information booths as appropriate. Information points may not operate overnight.

## Matching facilities to your visit

**Families and older visitors:** use station or shopping-center restrooms and shaded rest stops as route anchors every 30–40 minutes. Confirm step-free crossings before setting out with a wheelchair or stroller.

**Drivers:** prioritize the distance from a legal parking space to the waterfront, not simply the fee. Events and holidays may close roads or restrict vehicle access, including motorbike parking.

**Sunset photographers:** arrive early, park and eat before peak demand, and allow time to pay for parking and exit after dark.

**Short visits:** walking, MRT, light rail and shared bicycles often make more sense than driving around searching for a space.

## Safety and emergency help

Call **110** for police or **119** for fire and ambulance services in Taiwan. State the closest bridge, pier or station to help responders locate you. Stay on lit primary paths after dark and avoid isolated or wet areas outside safety railings.
